Physical oceanography, statistics and Mātauranga Māori in one practice

SEA-Mod combines state-of-the-art physical oceanography with machine-learning and stepwise statistical modelling to turn long wave records into decisions for coastal planning.

We work with Mātauranga Māori principles for sustainable coastal stewardship, and we state the uncertainty of every result so that councils and engineering partners can use it with confidence.

Services

  • Coastal hazard and inundation risk assessment

Case Study 1: Nearshore wave climate transformation (30 m to 10 m depth)

Proof-of-concept (ENVSCI704): we transformed offshore wave time series to nearshore conditions using 1993–2019 data, validated the models on the last 30% of that record, and extended the nearshore hindcast to 2020–2025.

Single-variable baseline. Linear regression predicts nearshore Hsig from offshore Hsig only: Hsig = 0.152 + 1.093 × H2.

Stepwise linear model. Starts from offshore Hsig, peak period and wave direction (as sine and cosine vector components, u and v) plus their pairwise interactions, then keeps only the terms that pass p-value thresholds (enter 0.05, remove 0.10). For Hsig it kept all four predictors and five interactions.

Validation. Trained on 1 January 1993 to 25 November 2011; tested on 25 November 2011 to 31 December 2019 (23,666 three-hourly records).
